Scheduling a solar microgrid with Evolutionary Programming

A rural health centre has solar panels, a battery and a diesel generator. The algorithm below, written from scratch with no optimisation libraries, learns an hour-by-hour plan that burns as little diesel as possible while the critical load is never left unpowered.

How it works

  1. One individual is one whole day. It holds 48 numbers: diesel output and battery power for each of 24 hours, plus a mutation step size for every number.
  2. Fitness is a simulation. The day is replayed hour by hour while tracking battery charge. Cost is diesel burned (0.27 L/kWh plus 0.6 L/h while running) times price, plus heavy penalties for unmet load, battery limits (20% to 100%) and ending the day below the starting charge.
  3. Mutation is the only variation. Each of 30 parents makes one child. Step sizes change first, σ' = σ·exp(τ'N + τNⱼ), then values move by σ'·N(0,1). Good step sizes survive along with good schedules, so the search tunes itself.
  4. Selection is a tournament. Parents and children (60) each face 10 random rivals and score a win for every rival they beat. The 30 with the most wins form the next generation.
  5. The random numbers are hand-made. A small seeded generator supplies uniform numbers and the Box-Muller transform turns them into Gaussian ones.
